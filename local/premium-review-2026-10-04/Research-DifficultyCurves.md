# Difficulty curves, fairness, and local pressure — evidence handback
Research date: 2026-10-05. Evidence only; no server settings proposed. Labels: **measured** = empirical study; **intent** = developer/practitioner argument or documented target; **observed code** = inspected implementation, not playtested. Sources are keyed below with exact locators.

## Summary
- Challenge–skill balance is a useful design framework, not a proven universal numerical optimum: Chen's 2007 ACM **Viewpoint** describes individual Flow Zones and anxiety/boredom boundaries, while a preregistered 2023 experiment (n=311) found no significant enjoyment or engagement difference across easy/balanced/hard conditions. [S1, pp.32–34; S3, §§3.2,4]
- A controlled 2016 study (n=93, 12-minute side-scroller sessions) found dynamic-high pacing produced more flow than constant balance, while dynamic-medium pacing produced more enjoyment than constant balance. Flow and enjoyment were experimentally distinct; this supports tension/release, not an indefinitely steep ramp. [S2, Table 2, “Pacing curves, flow, and enjoyment”]
- Relative budgets matter more than displayed stat size: Blizzard's 2014 stat-squish example preserves 15% monster-health damage per Fireball despite smaller numbers; its healing design explicitly addresses gear growth that otherwise forces increasingly bursty encounter damage. [S4, “Stat Squish”; S5, “Retuning Healing Spells”]
- Fairness is not reducible to a health/damage percentage: Mike Stout argues avoidance combat needs communicated attacks and a pre-attack delay; God of War's designers report that poor visibility caused panic and use aggression tokens to maintain manageable threats. These are practitioner/developer evidence, not a measured universal reaction-time threshold. [S6, “Pre—attack Animations”; S7, slides 29–32,37–44]
- Iron Gate explicitly says Valheim should become harder further into progression and recommends Queen completion before Ashlands; it does not publish a biome-by-biome TTK or damage-spike budget in that guidance. [S8, “Preparation and Difficulty”]
- Escalation systems have different scopes: Risk of Rain's timer deliberately creates urgency; PoE Delirium rewards deeper danger and allows map-intensity choice; Hades' Pact is a selectable run-condition system with rewards and staged introduction—not persistent shared regional kill pressure. [S9, “About This Game,” goals 2–3; S10, “Plunge into the Horror,” “Orbs of Delirium”; S11, Patch 032 “Pact of Punishment”]
- GGG's Delirium corrections document concrete failure modes: overly deadly stacked modifiers, insufficient warning/visibility, on-death explosions, and timers competing with other encounters. Its response combined damage reductions, fewer modifiers, cast warnings, visual differentiation, encounter pauses and improved deep rewards. [S12, “Monster Damage,” “Past Content Within Delirium,” “Visibility,” “Improving Delirium Monster Visuals”]
- No verified source here establishes a transferable maximum difficulty-growth percentage, safe one-shot frequency, or measured effect of shared regional escalation on early gatherers/late joiners. Those remain explicit gaps, not support for a numerical fairness claim. [S2–S3 scope; Gaps]

## 1. Difficulty relative to power through progression

### Challenge, skill, pacing, and curve shapes
**Intent / theoretical interpretation — Chen (2007).** *Flow in Games (and Everything Else)* is a four-page Communications of the ACM Viewpoint, not a controlled experiment. It attributes Flow's challenge/skill balance, clear goals, feedback, and sense of control to Csikszentmihalyi, *Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience* (1990). Its “In the Zone” section allows a fuzzy tolerance zone rather than exact equality and argues novices and experts require different choices embedded in core play. Constraint established: “player power” cannot stand in for learned player ability or personal challenge preference. The original Csikszentmihalyi book was not independently read; do not present Chen's interpretation as fresh measurement of the theory. [S1]

**Measured — Baumann, Lürig & Engeser (2016).** Random assignment to constant balance, dynamic medium and dynamic high pacing; n=93, 59 female, ages 19–61; *Sideway Runner*, PS Vita, 720 seconds. Complexity/ramping was held the same across conditions while speed/pacing differed. Dynamic medium spent 120 seconds low, 96 high, 24 rest per 240-second cycle; dynamic high spent 216 high, 24 rest. Bounds were 750 and 950 pixels/second, not transferable combat stat multipliers. Table 2 mean flow: 4.85 constant, 5.18 medium, 5.39 high; enjoyment: 4.39, 5.21, 4.53 respectively (seven-point scales). High vs constant flow and medium vs constant enjoyment were significant; medium vs high enjoyment was not significant. Condition-by-experience interaction: F(2,88)=4.15, p<.02, partial eta²=.086. Constraint: sawtooth-like tension/release has experimental support in this short task, but neither an optimal biome slope nor exact wave amplitude follows. [S2, “Game: sideway runner,” Fig.1, Table 2, Results]

**Contrary measured evidence — Cutting, Deterding, Demediuk & Sephton (2023).** Preregistered online tactical game study, n=311; two full 2–3-minute matches after tutorial. AI targeted player win margins +15,0,−15. Actual medians +18,−1,−15; easy players all won and hard players lost 94.1%. Enjoyment difference was not significant (F(2,308)=1.29,p=.277,partial eta²=.008); voluntary replay difference p=.183. Agency difference p=.230. Authors explicitly warn against treating lack of an agency difference as proof players never noticed adjustment; no non-DDA control or long-term progression condition was included. Constraint: this null finding does not erase the 2016 pacing result—the tasks, manipulation, timescale, outcomes and sample differ. It does undermine claiming one universal inverted-U optimum. [S3, Table 1, §§2–4]

**Practitioner book — Adams & Dormans (2012), *Game Mechanics: Advanced Game Design*.** Publisher sample provides Chapter 4, “Internal Economy,” pp.59–78. Health, experience, ammunition, time and enemies are resources; sources/drains/converters/traders express their rates. “Economic Shapes,” p.63 explicitly states there is no single graph shape identifying good gameplay; desired fortunes depend on design goals/context. Table of contents locates Chapter 8 “Simulating and Balancing Games” p.171, Chapter 11 “Progression Mechanisms” p.247 and Appendix B “Escalating Challenge” p.325; those chapters' bodies are not included in the sample and are not claimed as read. Constraint: a flat *relative* challenge can coexist with rising absolute numbers; economy graphs should not be mistaken for a universal fun curve. [S13]

**Schell requested attribution:** *The Art of Game Design*, first edition (2008), tension/release pp.121–122, is explicitly cited by S2's “Demand fluctuation.” This is a **secondary attribution to the practitioner book**, not direct verification of the figure or exact term “sawtooth.” Second-edition catalog locates Chapter 11 “Game Mechanics Must Be in Balance,” “Challenge vs. Success” and “Dynamic Game Balancing”; O'Reilly and ScienceDirect body access returned 403. No invented book quotation or page number for sawtooth is supplied. [S2; S14 access gap]

### Health/damage/gear budgets and TTK/time to die
**Intent — Blizzard 2014.** “Stat Squish” smooths obsolete expansion power spikes, makes power linear through levels 1–85, and changes creatures/abilities/gear together. Example: 450,000 damage against 3,000,000 health becomes 30,000 against 200,000, preserving 15%. It also says specializations' attack/spell-power scaling will be aligned. This is not a disclosed complete MMO item-budget equation. [S4]

**Intent — Blizzard 2014 healing.** Gear caused heals to restore a growing fraction of health, forcing increasingly bursty damage to remain challenging. Goal: players spend time below full health without imminent death; health and healing should scale similarly across the expansion, with weaker relative healing, triage and efficiency choices. The article separately doubles post-squish player health, offsets PvE creature damage/healing, and reduces PvP crit effects 200%→150%. PvP numbers must not be imported as PvE tuning evidence. Constraint: survivability includes recovery throughput, shields, resources and burst timing, not only DPS or average health. [S5]

**Intent / algorithm specification — Hunicke & Chapman 2004.** Hamlet estimates probability of a health shortfall from initial health and observed mean/variance of damage. Assumes stationary, uncorrelated damage; explicitly warns about small samples, prediction horizon and other distributions. Comfort policy targets approximately 50% health, dipping 25%/cresting 75%; discomfort policy 15–20% health. These are proposed example policies, not established fairness thresholds or validated MMO TTD budgets. Reactive in-play stat changes can make immediate obstacle behavior confusing; proactive off-stage changes can create uncertainty and cascading reactive corrections. [S15, “Damage,” “Shortfalls,” “Actions,” “Policies,” “Evaluation”]

**ARPG scaling changes — Diablo IV 2023.** Patch 1.1.0a (July 18), “World Tier”: WTIII/IV monsters can trail the player by up to five levels; exceptions include Helltide, Nightmare Dungeons, world bosses, Legion and Fields of Hatred. Developer note: demonstrate power progression/mastery while retaining structured endgame challenge. Patch 1.2.0 (October 17), “Monsters”: open-world monsters scale with player level within WT bounds (I/II 1–50, III 55–70, IV 75–100), and monsters/bosses were rebalanced around other changes to avoid drastically changing overall difficulty. This is a dated policy change, not a contradiction to be resolved by picking one version. Exact monster-health/DPS growth equation is absent. [S16]

**Valheim biome step — Iron Gate March 26, 2024.** Explicitly harder further through progression; Queen's item enables Ashlands progression, analogous to antler pickaxe and Elder crypt key. It offers practice/world-modifier adjustment for rusty players. This confirms design intent for harder later biomes, not constant relative difficulty or prescribed percentage jumps. August 27 patch 0.218.21 metadata says spawn rates and Flametal mining were adjusted to make Ashlands more enjoyable; fetched Steam body omitted the actual notes, so only the metadata claim is verified. [S8,S17]

**Analytical distinction [INFERENCE, not published target]:** enemy effective health / effective player DPS approximates TTK in a continuous sustained-damage model; player effective health / incoming post-mitigation DPS approximates time to die only absent healing, avoidance and burst variation. Keeping these ratios flat is different from keeping raw stats flat. No source in this handback establishes a number of seconds appropriate for this server.

## 2. Steepness, fairness, readability and one-shots

**No empirical universal slope located.** The studies above measure pacing or win margins, not an X% increase in enemy health/damage per biome with unfairness as a dependent variable. Their enjoyment/flow/agency measures must not be relabeled “fairness.” [S2,S3]

**Practitioner opinion — Mike Stout, September 2, 2015.** Avoidance combat asks “Can you avoid my damage?” and “Can you hit me?”; damage must first be signaled. Pre-attack animation delay gives a fair chance to respond; overlap of well-communicated questions creates challenge, rather than confusion. Appendix contrasts attrition systems where individual attacks take small health fractions and the collective question is whether players win before resources run out. Constraint: higher damage is not independently evidence of better or worse fairness; perception depends on available warning, tools and resource model. No measured wind-up minimum is given. [S6]

**Developer postmortem — Mihir Sheth, GDC 2019, God of War 2018.** Slides 29–32 report early players panicking/evading/mashing when the close camera obscured fight state; difficulty should arise from game, not controls/camera. Slides 37–44 describe enemy aggression scoring and fixed token pool, tuned by difficulty to keep manageable active threats. These are internal playtest observations without sample size or controlled statistics. Constraint: simultaneous attacks, visibility and target tracking can change pressure even when individual enemy stats remain identical. [S7]

**First-party correction — GGG March 19, 2020.** Delirium danger corrections included Rage damage −20%, stationary volatile damage −30%, no mortar impact damage, fewer modifiers, lower on-death effect frequency, redder/larger explosive entities and notable casts instead of instant contact explosions. Developers explicitly aimed to identify danger quickly and give players a better chance to counter while retaining challenge. Constraint: a 20–30% reduction here is a specific live correction, not a universal tolerable slope; visual warnings and stack frequency changed too, confounding any numerical-only interpretation. [S12]

**Health sponges / damage spikes:** S5 documents the throughput→burst escalation problem, and S6 distinguishes attrition and avoidance; neither directly measures how long a health sponge becomes boring or the acceptable one-shot probability. A lethal telegraphed attack and unavoidable lethal background damage are not interchangeable in these sources. Any numeric threshold remains unknown, especially after mods alter animations, stamina, recovery, armor and simultaneous attackers.

### Vanilla raid evidence and late-joiner boundary
Decompiled supplied assembly with `~/.dotnet/tools/ilspycmd -t RandEventSystem lib/valheim/assembly_valheim.dll`; scratch `/tmp/lembitu-premium/DifficultyCurves/RandEventSystem.cs`. Version 1.0.16 supplied by brief; assembly metadata not independently version-checked.
- `RandEventSystem.cs:132–139`: timer gated by `Game.m_eventRate`, interval and chance; source field initializers 27–29 are not certified runtime prefab settings.
- `:411–440`: eligible random events need enabled/random/key checks; event points require valid biome, CheckBase, altitude and optional player possible-event check.
- `:500–510`: `m_nearBaseOnly` requires `baseValue>=3`; otherwise base check passes.
- `:513–540`: optional PlayerEvents mode with alternate requirements can admit an event if a player's possible-event list includes it; otherwise required/not-required global keys determine eligibility.
**Inference:** global progression eligibility can expose less-progressed players to a globally eligible threat; optional per-player checks change this boundary. This is a reachable structural concern, not a measured late-joiner failure rate. Repository says world keys are replaced/written none (`CONTEXT.md:147–149`) and custom sieges replace vanilla random raids (`:184–187`), so vanilla behavior is an analogue, not a description of the modded server's current effective behavior.

### Known failures versus untested risks
**Documented first-party failures:** GGG changed Delirium interaction with old-content timers, excessive on-death damage, warning visibility and rewards together. Hades changed Tight Deadline and mini-boss/first-attempt pressure contexts. Hunicke & Chapman warn automatic adjustments can spiral, disrupt stable obstacle interpretation, or trivialize gameplay; these are design risks, not reported measured frequencies. [S12,S11,S15]

**Unverified for this local server:** early gathering becoming dangerous because unavoidable defensive kills build pressure; a strong player's activity burdening a new player; repeated high-pressure spawns creating yet more pressure when killed; relocation shifting burden to another guild; intentionally dying to lower pressure. These are **[INFERENCE] causal risks implied by the brief**, not established external observations. No verified experiment here measures them. Need actual trigger/attribution/decay code and real sessions to settle them.

**Not verified analogues:** Remnant official-site searches found world generation descriptions, not a regional activity-pressure specification. No primary-source notoriety/heat implementation was established here, so neither Remnant nor generic crime heat is claimed equivalent to Karma.
