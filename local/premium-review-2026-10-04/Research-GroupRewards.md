# Group rewards evidence — GroupRewards

Research date: 2026-10-05. Evidence only; no server-setting recommendations. No game runtime was exercised. **Intent** means developer-stated rationale; **measured** means empirical study; **report** means documented mechanics/community observation, not independently measured here. Officially hosted community wikis remain community evidence. Numbers derived from source formulas are marked arithmetic, not telemetry.

## Summary

1. A party-pool bonus is not a per-player XP bonus: the historical WoW community formula gives total multipliers 1/1/1.166/1.3/1.4 for 1–5 players, hence 28% of solo kill XP each at five equal-level players. The source explicitly calls its testing limited and its formulas a best guess. [S1, Group Experience](https://wowpedia.fandom.com/wiki/Mob_experience#Group_Experience).
2. FFXI's reported normal shares are 100/60/45/40/37/35% per player at sizes 1–6, equivalent to total pools 100/120/135/160/185/210%; Signet changes small-party shares. This is historical community documentation with an outdated-table warning, not verified retail code. [S2, Player Share](https://ffxiclopedia.fandom.com/wiki/Experience_Points#Player_Share).
3. Ragnarok's official guide documents +25% total XP per additional **attacker**, even share on the same map, a ten-level spread limit, and no XP for dead characters. This conflicts with later Renewal community reports of +20% per party member/15-level range; the guide is undated and cannot establish current Renewal behaviour. [S3, Sharing Experience in a Party](https://renewal.playragnarok.com/gameguide/howtoplay_basicsystem05.aspx).
4. Lineage II Chronicle 3 explicitly increased small-party XP bonuses, e.g. two members +7%→+30%, four +23%→+50%, to improve small-party hunting. Exact modern/Classic rules cannot be substituted for that historical version. [S4, party experience paragraph](https://legacy-lineage2.com/news/chronicle3_06.html).
5. GW2 rewards need not be split or increased to support cooperation: its official-hosted community wiki says solo and party rewards are the same, with easier party tagging; DRG documents full team XP for every player and a **credit**, not XP, survival award of 150 base credits per surviving dwarf. Both also scale combat, so neither is a fixed-difficulty whole-system precedent. [S5, Notes](https://wiki.guildwars2.com/wiki/Party#Notes); [S6, Obtaining XP](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Experience#Obtaining_XP); [S7, Survival Bonus](https://deeprockgalactic.wiki.gg/wiki/Credits).
6. No universal percentage needed to change grouping behaviour was established. Yee measured motivational factors, not XP-bonus dose response; Ikariam research measured 1,572,734 contribution decisions by 134,631 players and found lower contributions when those same players were newcomers (about 14%) than incumbents (about 32%), not an optimal party bonus. [S8, Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17201605); [S9, Differences in contribution behavior](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34160-5).
7. Blizzard's 2012 personal-loot rationale explicitly removed competition over loot to permit adding strangers without reducing friends' chances, while retaining boss-group participation eligibility. The stated aim was less social pressure, **not** faster gearing. [S10, Personal Loot](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/4736886/dev-watercooler-mists-of-pandaria-looting-explained).
8. Blizzard's 2022 raid rationale favoured visible rolls, free trading to eligible looters and social allocation, with class/spec restrictions; Jagex's 2016 lootshare proposal warned that damage-only allocation excludes support roles, visible lootshare potential encourages group-shopping, and decaying potential encourages nonstop play. These are intent/risk statements, not measured carrying rates. [S11, posts 23/363](https://www.bluetracker.gg/wow/topic/us-en/1352473-changes-experiments-to-raid-rewards-in-dragonflight-season-1/); [S12, What is lootshare?/Visible/Decaying potential](https://forums.rs/en/380,381,261,65740509.html).

## 1. Exact party XP rules and reward magnitudes

### Normalizing the denominator

Let E be an eligible solo kill's XP, n eligible recipients, B(n) the total pool multiplier, and K(n) kills per unit time. Equal split yields E·B(n)/n per kill per player. **Arithmetic:** relative per-player XP rate is [K(n)/K(1)]·B(n)/n, before travel, formation, downtime and level corrections. Thus a 150% total pool at four recipients is 37.5% of solo kill XP each, not +50% each. A group must kill more than 4/1.5 = 2.667 times as fast to exceed the solo rate under those assumptions. This is an identity, not a behavioural finding or server recommendation. WoW and FFXI reports below are examples of why the distinction matters [S1–S2].

### Source constraints, not settings advice

- The evidence distinguishes **pool inflation**, **independent full rewards**, **allocation of a scarce shared drop**, and **targeted helper incentives**. Those cannot be compared as interchangeable percentages [S1–S7, S13–S20].
- Fixed difficulty is not established for every requested game. GW2 and DRG explicitly scale combat [S5, S26]. Their reward systems still demonstrate non-rival rewards but cannot prove the behaviour of fixed-strength bosses.
- Proximity/level/activity constraints are part of each rule, not incidental details: Ragnarok counts additional attackers and excludes dead characters; FFXIV excludes already-penalized/unrestricted situations; old RuneScape's radius is not its modern radius [S3, S13–S14, S17–S19].

## 2. Behavioural magnitude and side effects

### What the research actually measured

**Yee (2006), Motivations for Play in Online Games**, CyberPsychology & Behavior 9(6), 772–775, DOI 10.1089/cpb.2006.9.772 [S8]. **Measured:** the reachable primary abstract states a factor-analytic empirical motivation model, ten subcomponents grouped into achievement, social and immersion, and relationships with age/gender/usage. It does **not** report a party-XP intervention or required bonus magnitude. Indexed author-PDF claims about roughly 3,000 respondents and 40 survey items were found, but the paper's full text repeatedly timed out; those counts are **not independently verified in this handback**. The abstract is sufficient to establish what type of measurement it was, not numerical bonus policy.

**Ducheneaut, Yee, Nickell & Moore (2006), “Alone Together?” Exploring the Social Dynamics of Massively Multiplayer Online Games**, CHI 2006 pp.407–416, DOI 10.1145/1124772.1124834 [S27]. Correct authors include Yee and Nickell; not simply Ducheneaut & Moore. Search indexing identifies longitudinal WoW playtime/grouping/guild analysis, not a randomized XP experiment. Full text could **not** be re-reached: author PDF timeout (multiple encoded/host variants and curl), ACM 403, ResearchGate/DeepDyve 403, SciSpace CAPTCHA, PARC redirected to SRI. Consequently exact sample/window/grouping percentages and solo-efficiency claims are **documented gaps**, not findings. This paper must not be cited here for “a 50% bonus causes grouping,” or any exact causal threshold.

**Otten et al. (2022), Human cooperation in changing groups in a large-scale public goods game**, Nature Communications 13:6399, DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-34160-5 [S9]. **Measured observational:** Ikariam sawmill investment. Figure 3 includes **1,572,734 contribution decisions, 134,631 players**. Observations organized into **28 biweekly intervals**; about 11.3k island groups/234k composition changes. The outcome is fraction of available resources contributed, not boss damage, party presence or XP. Within-player analysis: about **14% contributed as newcomers vs 32% as incumbents**, rising toward 40% after ten periods. Results/Discussion interpret socialization to group norms; correlations/controls do not randomize membership changes. Non-contributors still benefit from the public good, which is non-excludable/non-rival. **Scope limit:** persistent strategy islands (up to 17 players), not an eight-friend action-RPG combat party or adjustable XP schedule.

**Dindar & Akbulut (2014), Motivational characteristics of Turkish MMORPG players**, Computers in Human Behavior 33:119–125, DOI 10.1016/j.chb.2014.01.016 [S28]. **Measured self-report:** adaptation of Yee's 39-item scale, **307** usable respondents from two Turkish-interface MMORPGs, confirmatory factor analysis. Table 5: advancement mean **4.336**, teamwork **2.614** on the survey scale. Table 3 teamwork items explicitly include preference for group vs solo, enjoying group work, and solo/self-sufficient character importance. This is contrary to assuming all social-game players mainly value teamwork. Discussion states motivations overlap, and cautions that self-report/context limits inference. Some subscale reliabilities are low (teamwork alpha **.522**). This paper is not Yee's original despite the mirror filename. Neither it nor Yee establishes how much external reward converts a solo-preferring player.

| Group-shopping / weak-group preference | Same blog warns visible lootshare potential/rank may cause players to leave until they find a group where their own rank is highest [S12, Visible Lootshare Potential]. | A documented exploit incentive in reward allocation, not a measured test of behaviour change. |

**Magnitude conclusion (coverage boundary):** neither the requested seminal MMO studies nor the reached design documents establish a cross-game numerical threshold. A percentage divorced from split denominator, kill-rate improvement, party formation time and eligibility cannot even identify whether grouping is personally profitable. The quantitative identity in question 1 and the attribution table are evidence boundaries, not settings recommendations.

## 3. Shared versus per-player boss loot and late carries

### Blizzard 2012: remove a barrier to adding players

**Intent / primary developer statement**, Greg Street (“Ghostcrawler”), lead systems designer, 2012-03-27 [S10, Personal Loot]:

- Current Raid Finder: roll shared items, possibly with AFK strangers, weak social consequences, then drama.
- Proposed Mists system: **each player's loot chance independent of others**, winning item appropriate to class/current spec; no Need/Greed/Pass and no trading in this initial design.
- Explicit goal: reduce social pressure among anonymous strangers, **not** make boss farming/getting loot more efficient.
- Friends/guilds discussing allocation remains reasonable and is described as an RPG tradition.
- World bosses: adding a stranger should not take friends' loot; “the more the merrier.” Avoid smallest-possible groups chosen to maximize loot per person.
- Still requires membership in the group that taps/kills; explicitly not credit for everyone merely lurking in the zone.

**Scope:** initial 2012 design for Raid Finder/world bosses, not all subsequent personal-loot algorithms. “Personal loot” does not mean guaranteed one item per participant, nor universally independent probabilities in every later version.

### Blizzard 2022: restore choice within the allocation process, not loot-mode choice

**Intent / preserved primary statements**, Scariizard, WoW developer, 2022-09-29, Dragonflight S1 [S11]. Original Blizzard thread now 404; Blue Tracker preserves dated posts:

- Post 1: Vault of the Incarnates uses mandatory Group Loot; raid item-level trade restrictions removed. Some reagents remain personal.
- Post 23: personal-loot armor/weapon stacking had improved targeted drop odds; global boss table now means a lone rogue has equal opportunity to see an upgrade, but an unusable bow can drop without a hunter. Developer explicitly says it rewards class diversity.
- Post 363: class equip restriction, main-spec > off-spec > greed, duplicate-win protection, exact-piece/exact-ilvl Need restriction with upgrade exceptions; trades only to **eligible** looters not already weekly-locked to boss.
- Same post: personal loot previously rolled invisibly; visible Group Loot gives transparency and freedom to allocate socially. Acknowledges LFR friction and possibility of increasing quantities if excessive.

**Contrary evidence / scope distinction:** 2012 prioritized avoiding anonymous-stranger social negotiation; 2022 prioritized transparency/social allocation even in LFR. These are different dated design intents. They do not prove one universal best loot mode. User forum responses from 2022 [S30, posts 3/4/19] foresee drama versus argue new restrictions might suffice; **reports/opinions**, not outcome measurements.

### Jagex: redistribute a fixed drop versus duplicate rewards

**Intent / preserved primary blog**, Mod Kieren and Old School team, 2016-01-29 [S12]: share fixed loot value so support participants can be rewarded. Original randomness made high potential unreliable; visibility might worsen frustration/group-shopping; decay may pressure continued play; old CoinShare injected coins if automatic exchange offers never sold. These are distinct economy/behaviour problems, not proof of a released OSRS solution.

**Report / current RS3 community documentation** [S18–S19]: LootShare redistributes items and future chances; CoinShare divides 120 shards immediately. Wiki says long-run LootShare fairness depends on staying for multiple valuable drops, whereas shards let temporary teams split one rare then leave. The same wiki discourages CoinShare because shards do not normally fill collection logs and can be inconvenient to sell; that judgement is community advice, not developer intent.

### What is established about carrying late arrivals

- **Removing dilution is explicitly intended to make bringing an additional player less costly** in 2012 world bosses [S10]. This supports inclusion, not a measured claim about increased selling of carries.
- **Group allocation can explicitly funnel tradable gear to eligible players** under 2022 rules [S11, post 363]. That can include undergeared participants; actual carrying frequency or speed was not measured by the source.
- Late arrival **in the fight** versus late arrival **in the campaign** are separate cases. Boss eligibility/lockouts regulate the former; neither Blizzard statement establishes that newly arriving characters can skip all progression requirements.
- **Inference, not telemetry:** a fixed shared item pool dilutes expected shares when another equally eligible claimant joins; independent non-rival rewards remove that dilution. Ability to transfer items can accelerate recipient gear acquisition without transferring skill. Exact item count/drop chance/contribution threshold is required before quantifying that acceleration. No universal carry rate follows from loot-mode names.
- Official Ragnarok MVP guide instead singles out strongest contributor, excluding death [S3, MVP System]; GW2 requires contribution but gives each eligible player rewards [S16]. Both are contrary examples to an unconditional “everyone present gets full loot” rule.
- Monster Hunter World's commonly reported 10-minute late-join rule was located in community discussion, but official World manual redirected/broke and no primary passage was verified. It is intentionally **not** treated as settled here.

## Gaps

1. **Causal magnitude:** no reached randomized experiment/telemetry varying MMO party XP bonus and measuring group formation, active cooperation, AFK, retention or solo dissatisfaction. A version-specific developer A/B report with eligibility/denominator controls would settle it. Search-generated proposed experiments and unsupported hypothetical thresholds were excluded.
2. **Alone Together full text:** multiple author URL variants/curl timed out; ACM/ResearchGate/DeepDyve blocked; SciSpace CAPTCHA; PARC redirected. Need an accessible authentic PDF/library copy to verify measurement window, sample, grouping percentages and efficiency comparisons. Its DOI/title/authorship are identified; exact study findings remain unverified.
3. **Yee original full text:** primary abstract reached, author/CiteSeer PDFs not. Need authentic full text to verify original sample/question count; no original full-text statistics asserted.
4. **FFXI/WoW mechanics:** exact tables are community reports (WoW explicitly tentative); no official numeric party table/code reached. Need era-specific official manual or reproducible controlled observations. FFXI proximity radius and all modern XP modes unresolved; WoW historical formula is not current-version proof.
5. **FFXIV:** official 3.2 confirms rules but not 50% in extracted text; current +50%/90 cap verified only in community documentation. No generic party-size XP multiplier verified. Need official buff datum or patch passage for numeric percentage/current cap.
6. **Lineage Classic:** indexed 2018 +60–120% table cannot be re-reached at original now-redirected URL; no allocation/proximity/member-level distribution established. C3 evidence directly read and version-separated. Need official archived launch notes and distribution rules; indexed table not decision-grade.
7. **Ragnarok:** undated official guide +25%/attacker/10-level limit versus 2011–2012 Renewal reports +20%/member/15-level limit. Need dated region/server patch notes; no current universal rule inferred.
8. **RS3/OSRS:** no shared RS3 combat-XP formula primary-verified; OSRS ordinary XP documented only secondarily. Historical 16-square LootShare versus current unspecified/20-space CoinShare separated. Boss-specific loot and group-ironman/campaign catches require boss/version evidence; 2016 proposal does not prove shipping.
9. **Monster Hunter:** World zenny split secondarily verified; Capcom World manual returned a broken route; MH4U manual does not establish World item/HR split or late-join thresholds. Need working official World manual/game observation; no generic series XP bonus inferred.
10. **DRG/GW2:** official community documentation, not extracted code/developer numeric specification. Exact participation radii, late-join and anti-AFK thresholds unresolved. DRG survival award is credits, not XP. Both scale combat and are not fixed-difficulty equivalents.
11. **Carry effects:** no reached comparative study measuring late-campaign carries under shared/personal loot. Primary intents plus eligibility mechanics establish incentives/opportunities, not rates or player attitudes.
